# FASHION MEETS AI — Editorial Master Brief V5

**Status:** Editorial direction for approval. This brief supersedes earlier editorial outlines and manuscripts when there is a conflict. It does NOT erase previous versions, research, citations or layout assets. No PDF/layout work until the content sample is approved.

## 1. What we are making

A compelling, accurate, sophisticated **Hebrew fashion-magazine feature** about the transformation of the fashion industry through artificial intelligence and technology. It should surprise fashion-interested readers, make complex developments accessible, and tell concrete stories. It is **not** a patternmaking guide, a sewing article, a generic overview of AI, or a dry academic report.

**Reader experience:** "לא תיארתי לעצמי שטכנולוגיה ובינה מלאכותית משפיעות כבר עכשיו על כל כך הרבה שלבים בעולם האופנה — מעיצוב הבגד, דרך חיזוי הטרנדים ועד הקמפיינים והקנייה."

**Central editorial question:** How has AI changed the way fashion is conceived, forecast, presented, and consumed, and what comes next?

**Chapter independence:** The four chapters are independent features within the shared theme of Fashion Meets AI. Give each its own subject, opening, research, case studies, editorial rhythm, and ending. Build coherence within each chapter. Do not link the chapters through a central storyline, recurring metaphor, garment, example, idea used as a narrative device, or artificial handoffs. Reuse a case across chapters only when it is indispensable to a different point. Give each ending a specific insight earned by that chapter rather than repeating a general conclusion about AI and human judgment.

**Abstract approach:** Build the overview around the perceived distance between fashion and technology. Begin with fashion as art, creativity, aesthetics, emotion, and personal expression, and technology as science, logic, algorithms, and data. Show how closely those worlds are connected in practice and how broadly technology is changing fashion. Introduce the four independent subjects briefly and vividly, with technical explanations and case-study detail reserved for their chapters. The abstract is an introduction to the issue and should occupy about three quarters of a print page, meeting the existing 75% target at layout.

**Voice:** natural, fluent, elegant, vivid, journalistic Hebrew; knowledgeable without condescension. The driving force is an interesting reality or case, not repetitive metaphors, speculation, or abstract warnings. Each chapter must deliver rich specifics rather than filler.

## 3. Required narrative within each chapter

### Chapter 1 — From sketch to collection: how does technology change fashion design?
**Page 5** — Explain the human workflow in a vivid but concise manner: inspiration, sketch, technical sketch, pattern, prototype/fitting, production specification. The aim is to establish a clear before-picture, **not** to teach sewing or construction techniques.

**Page 6** — Show the technological changes step-by-step: digital design tools, 3D fitting/prototyping where supported, generative AI for concepts or specifications. Clarify what each tool actually does and which tasks still require physical verification. Concrete workflows and practical implications matter more than jargon.

**Page 7** — Deep, sourced case study of **Genpire**: problem addressed, workflow before/after, actual features demonstrated, who would use it and why. Separate vendor claims from independent validation. End with an insight about speed and creativity, not generic AI ethics.

### Chapter 2 — Can anyone predict what we will wear next?
**Page 8** — Who are fashion forecasters and why do major brands rely on them? How they historically studied runway, stores, streets, culture, colors and consumer behavior, and translated observations into seasonal bets.

**Page 9** — What changes with machine learning/AI: visual recognition of garments, signals from social platforms, searches, catalog and commerce data; explain **WGSN and Heuritech** and contrast their documented approaches accurately. Make this understandable to a reader who loves fashion but is not a data scientist.

**Page 10** — A documented forecasting case or research example. Explain what exactly was forecast, what was measured, and why brands care; include failure modes and explain why trend popularity and sales are different, without allowing mathematical notation to dominate the page.

### Chapter 3 — The model who may not exist: the new age of fashion campaigns
**Page 11** — The old production workflow: creative concept, casting, contracts, model, photographer, makeup and styling, locations, travel, lighting, editing, timeline and budget categories. Make the complexity tangible. No invented budgets.

**Page 12** — How AI workflows can change it: text/image concepts, compositing, image generation, virtual sets, digital twins, model licensing, review and iteration. Distinguish fully synthetic campaign from hybrid production. Provide comparison across same deliverables, not deceptive savings figures.

**Page 13** — International case study: **H&M digital twins**, including what the company actually did, the creative result, participating people, and documented debate. Focus primarily on the innovation and workflow, with proportionate discussion of rights.

**Page 14** — Israeli case studies: **Renuar** and **Derococo** (research Derococo separately and do not assume it used AI without credible verification). Explain what specifically happened and how each case differs, with images or assets only if reuse is permitted.

**Page 15** — AI influencer / persistent virtual persona: **Super-Pharm’s Maya Lor** case, verified details about launch, ongoing content and reported expenditure with accurate context. Close with changes to jobs, advertising creativity and consumer trust. Do not conflate the cost of creating a long-term persona with a single photo shoot.

If verification does not support a named case, say so explicitly, suggest a well-documented substitute, and request editorial approval instead of inventing facts.

### Chapter 4 — What will fashion look like next?
**Page 16** — Digital fitting rooms, sizing and visualization, virtual try-on, guided shopping; distinguish practical deployment from marketing demos. Show how customer experience changes.

**Page 17** — Personalization, design/production planning and forecasting demand; link AI to stock decisions and possible waste reduction **without automatically claiming an environmental benefit**.

**Page 18** — Human and creative futures: designers, stylists, photographers, production teams, buyers, new professional skills and experiences. Use grounded scenarios and the latest verifiable developments, clearly labeled as present versus plausible future.

## 4. Research and quality rules

- **Case studies must answer:** Who did what? When? With which technology? How did the workflow differ? What evidence supports the claim? Why should a fashion reader care?
- Prefer primary sources, reputable trade publications, company documentation clearly identified as such, and scholarly work when relevant. Source every significant quantitative or disputed claim.
- Explicitly distinguish independently verified facts, company claims, media-reported figures, and editorial interpretations. No fabricated quotes, budgets, timelines or technical capabilities.
- Use the existing V4 sources as research leads, **not as an instruction to preserve V4 prose**. Research additional cases when they are required by this brief, especially Derococo.
- Visual assets may be recommended, but copyright, licensing and permission must be addressed. Do not insert unlicensed images as though they are cleared.
- All Hebrew writing and final RTL layout must use **Heebo**. Keep factual writing separate from design/production commands.

## 5. Writing quality gates

Every substantive page should have:
- A distinctive reader-interest hook or revealing example (not a forced metaphor).
- A concrete, accurate description of a practice/technology/case.
- A clear **before → change → significance** thread.
- Enough detailed, sourced information to fill a magazine page meaningfully — never word count padding.
- Varied narrative rhythm, natural transitions, economical jargon and nonrepetitive conclusions.

**Prohibitions:** No invented denim jacket or single fictional object as the narrative spine. No repeated 'תפר' metaphor in headings or abstract. No page of detailed sewing construction, endless rhetorical questions, generic "AI is changing everything" claims, or ethics disclaimers crowding out the innovation. Do not mechanically duplicate the same paragraph structure on every page. This is a magazine article, not a technical checklist.

## 7. Acceptance criteria (not just compile-success)

The final manuscript is approvable only if: (1) all four chapters and page counts match; (2) the abstract actually previews the interesting story; (3) fashion design history is background, not the main plot; (4) trend forecasting receives a rich three pages; (5) AI campaigns and virtual figures receive the detailed five pages requested, with clear before/after workflows and cases; (6) the future section is inspiring but evidence-aware; (7) every key example is verified or openly marked unverified; (8) the Hebrew reads like strong professional editorial writing; (9) artwork and attribution are handled responsibly. A clean 20-page PDF alone does **not** satisfy editorial quality.
